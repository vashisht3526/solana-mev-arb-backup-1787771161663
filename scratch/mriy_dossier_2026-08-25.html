Correction to our own earlier reasoning

At our minimum confirmed profit of 120k lamports, the V41 formula bids ~72k — already above yesterday's network-wide p95. The 1.5M ceiling is ~7× p99. Conclusion: tips were never why our 146 historical bundles failed to land; delivery timing and process freezes were. Overbidding costs nothing when reverted — it only leaves retention on the table when we win.

Mechanics that actually decide winners

  • Auctions run as parallel local auctions grouped by account write-lock, at 50 ms ticks, ranked by tip ÷ requested CU — fat compute requests lower rank and will cost extra once SIMD-0553 resource-fee gates activate in Agave 4.3.
  • Rate limits are per region (default ~1 req/s/IP/region): firing the same bundle at Amsterdam + Frankfurt + Dublin simultaneously multiplies attempts; losers expire harmlessly.
  • getInflightBundleStatuses diagnoses every failure for free: Invalid = construction bug, pending-until-expiry = missed leader window, Failed = lost auction.
  • Embed the tip transfer inside the arb transaction itself; randomize across the 8 tip accounts.
  • Harmonic accepts Jito-compatible bundles and takes no 5% cut — but permanently bans consistently-reverting submitters, so our simulation gate must stay strict.

Two clocks to watch

  • 2026-09-05 — Jito ShredStream full shutdown; sub-10 ms tier moves to DoubleZero Edge fiber seats.
  • Agave 4.3 / SIMD-0553 — governance voting through ~epoch 1023 (Aug 27, 2026): flat 2,500-lamport inclusion fee plus a 100%-burned resource fee on requested CUs, ramping to 0.5 lamports/CU. Tight CU budgets become money.

The honest reachable market

With event-driven triggers on feeds we already pay for, detect-to-submit lands around 15–50 ms. Worthwhile cross-DEX spreads average 0.2–0.5% lasting 3–15 s; the Jito relayer's 200 ms speed-bump is a structural window. A 15–50 ms reactor harvests everything persisting >200 ms and concedes only the sub-100 ms races — which are MRiY's paywalled world anyway.

Open-source templates exist for exactly this wiring — AV1080p/Solana-Arbitrage-Bot (Yellowstone-driven, hand-built pool cache) and jito-labs/shredstream-proxy's deshred example. Common pattern: pool delta arrives → recompute reserves locally → simulate route in memory → submit bundle, zero REST calls. One hardware note: the t3.small's burstable CPU credits are our weakest link once event-driven — a fixed-performance instance (~$30–120/mo) is a cheap tail-latency win.

The Spread Truth

track iv · empirical reality

The baseline numbers every expectation should be anchored to: 90.4 million successful arbitrages analyzed by Helius averaged $1.58 each before tips; Chorus One found atomic-arb opportunities in 51.6% of slots worth a median of $0.001–$0.009; even Paladin — a bot integrated into validators — captured only 15.84% of the value available to it.

Why spreads lie to scanners like ours

  • Top-of-book ≠ fill. Jupiter's own engineering post shows liquidity turning over within a single block — quotes and executions answer different questions.
  • >5% spreads are almost always phantoms — dead liquidity, stale indexing, or impersonation mismatches. Our +7%/+10% sightings sit exactly there.
  • Virtual liquidity lies: CLMM depth can overstate real reserves ~23×; documented case of $49M virtual vs $2.1M actual.
  • Risk premia masquerade as spreads: freeze authority, mint authority, Token-2022 transfer fees — one token quoted $0.002 while effectively costing $1.30.
  • PropAMMs reprice the reference: HumidiFi/SolFi/Tessera hold >80% of SOL-stablecoin flow and quote CEX-tight spreads; everything passive looks permanently "dislocated" against them.

Scanner filters that kill phantoms before they cost quote calls

  • Simulate the actual 0.15 SOL size through each pool's swap math with both legs' fees — never vault ratios or mid-prices.
  • Sanity band: reject >~5% (phantom) and <~1% (below our cost floor).
  • Depth gates: both pools' withdrawable liquidity ≥ 50–100× trade size; reject venue asymmetry >50× (impersonation signature).
  • Real SPL account balances only, not virtual liquidity; exclude Token-2022 transfer-fee mints unless modeled.
  • Safety screen: mint + freeze authorities revoked, present on Jupiter's strict list. If Jupiter won't route it, the spread is a honeypot quote.
  • Freshness gate: discard candidates computed from state >1 slot old.
  • Treat live Jupiter quotes as a lower bound, not truth; suspect PropAMM-containing routes.
  • Per-candidate EV accounting: P(land) × (edge − tip − fees) − P(fail) × failure cost. Track realized landing rate per route — that number is the business metric, not scanner spread.

What remains monetizable at our size

  • Volatility-event windows — launches, listings, sudden SOL moves — accepting low landing rates against better-piped snipers.
  • Neglected long-tail pairs that pass the safety screen — understanding their persistence usually is the risk premium.
  • Not much else. Triangular SOL→Token→USDC→SOL exists on-chain (cyclic arb grew to 40%+ of aggregator volume, closing in 400–800 ms) but every documented retail attempt loses small amounts; the USDC leg belongs to PropAMMs, where our edge is thinnest.

Honest Expectations

Executed perfectly, items 1–5 make us a competent harvester of multi-second windows and uncontested long-tail routes at 0.15 SOL size — realistically a pennies-to-low-dollars-per-day game until bankroll grows past the 0.248 SOL reserve-floor ceiling. The two things that separate $49K-quarter operators from everyone else are private validator lanes (regime-dependent, possibly temporary) and PropAMM flow access (structural). We can't have either yet. What we can have — event-driven reactions, direct routing, disciplined tips, honest filters — is exactly what the 21%-profitable cohort lacks, and it costs almost nothing but engineering time.
